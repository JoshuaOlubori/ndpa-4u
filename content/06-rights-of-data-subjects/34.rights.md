A data subject has the right to obtain confirmation from a data controller about whether their personal data is being processed. If it is, they have the right to know the purposes of processing, the categories of data involved, and the recipients of that data, especially those in other countries or international organizations.

Where possible, the data subject should be informed of the storage period or the criteria used to determine it. They also have the right to request the rectification, erasure, or restriction of their personal data, or to object to its processing. Furthermore, they can lodge a complaint with the Commission and, if the data wasn't collected directly from them, receive information about its source.

Data subjects are entitled to receive a copy of their personal data in a commonly used electronic format. However, if this creates unreasonable costs, the data subject may be required to cover some or all of those expenses. They also have the right to have inaccurate, out-of-date, or incomplete data corrected or deleted if correction is not feasible.

A data controller must erase personal data without undue delay if it is no longer necessary for its original purpose or if there is no other lawful basis for its retention. Additionally, data subjects can request a restriction of processing while a request or objection is being resolved, or for the establishment and defense of legal claims.
