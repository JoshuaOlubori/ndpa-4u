If a personal data breach occurs at a data processor, they must immediately notify the data controller and provide all necessary information to help the controller meet its obligations. The data controller, in turn, must notify the Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of any breach likely to risk the rights and freedoms of individuals.

If a breach poses a high risk to a data subject's rights and freedoms, the data controller must immediately communicate the breach to them in clear language. this communication should include advice on how to mitigate adverse effects. If direct notification is not feasible, a public announcement through widely used media may be used.

The notice to the Commission and the data subject must include contact details for further information, describe the likely consequences of the breach, and outline the measures taken or proposed to address and mitigate the issue.

The Commission has the authority to make its own public communications about a breach if it deems the controller's efforts inadequate. It will also issue regulations defining the steps controllers must take to inform data subjects properly.

When evaluating risks, both the controller and the Commission consider existing technical protections like encryption, any subsequent mitigation efforts, and the sensitivity of the data involved. All breaches must be documented, including facts, effects, and remedial steps, to allow for compliance verification.
