An adequate level of protection is one that upholds principles substantially similar to those provided in this Act. When assessing adequacy, factors such as the rule of law, the availability of enforceable data subject rights, and the existence of an independent supervisory authority with enforcement powers are considered.

The assessment also takes into account any instruments between the Commission and the recipient jurisdiction, the access public authorities have to data, and any international commitments or membership in regional organizations. The Commission will issue guidelines on how these factors should be evaluated.

The Commission may determine whether a country, region, sector, or set of standard contractual clauses provides adequate protection. It can also approve binding corporate rules or certification mechanisms if they meet appropriate standards. importantly, the absence of a specific determination by the Commission does not imply that the protections in place are adequate. The Commission may also base its determinations on adequacy decisions from other jurisdictions that consider similar factors.
