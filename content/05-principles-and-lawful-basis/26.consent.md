The data controller bears the burden of proof for establishing that a data subject has given consent. In determining if consent was freely given, it must be considered whether the performance of a contract or service is made conditional on consent for data processing that isn't necessary for that contract.

Silence or inactivity does not constitute consent. Where processing is based on consent, the data subject must be informed of their right to withdraw it before granting it. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing that occurred beforehand.

Requests for consent must be in clear, simple language and an accessible format. Consent itself must be an affirmative action, not based on pre-selected confirmations, and can be provided in writing, orally, or through electronic means.
