Data controllers and processors are generally prohibited from processing sensitive personal data unless the data subject has consented. Processing is also allowed if it's necessary for performing employment or social security obligations, or to protect the vital interests of a person who is incapable of giving consent.

Non-profit organizations with charitable, religious, or similar purposes may process such data for their members or regular contacts, provided the data isn't disclosed externally without explicit consent. Processing is also permitted for legal claims, substantial public interest based on law, medical care, community welfare, public health, or archiving in the public interest.

The Commission may issue regulations or directives prescribing further categories of sensitive data, additional grounds for processing, and necessary safeguards. In doing so, the Commission will consider the risk of significant harm, expectations of confidentiality, and the adequacy of general data protection.
