A data controller or data processor must ensure that personal data is processed fairly, lawfully, and transparently. Data should be collected for specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not processed in any way incompatible with those purposes.

The data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the stated purposes. It should be retained only as long as required to achieve those lawful bases. Furthermore, personal data must be accurate, complete, not misleading, and kept up to date when necessary.

Processing must ensure appropriate security, protecting against unauthorized access, loss, destruction, or any form of data breach. Data controllers and processors are required to use technical and organizational measures to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data.

A duty of care is owed in respect of data processing, and accountability must be demonstrated regarding these principles. When assessing the compatibility of further processing, factors such as the relationship between purposes, the nature of the data, consequences, and existing safeguards are considered. Further processing for archiving in the public interest, or for scientific, historical, or statistical research, is not considered incompatible with initial purposes.
